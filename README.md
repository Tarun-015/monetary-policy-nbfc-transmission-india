# Monetary Policy Transmission to India's NBFC Sector

### Funding Constraints, Transmission Lags and Policy Implications

**Research Period:** FY2019–FY2024  
**Geographic Focus:** India  
**Research Area:** Monetary Economics, Financial Economics, NBFCs and Monetary Policy

## Overview

Monetary policy transmission in India is not uniform across financial institutions. While changes in the Reserve Bank of India's (RBI) repo rate influence the broader financial system, their effects on Non-Banking Financial Companies (NBFCs) may operate indirectly through funding costs, liquidity conditions, and market risk premiums.

This research examines the relationship between monetary policy changes and NBFC lending behaviour in India. It focuses on whether changes in the repo rate are associated with changes in lending growth, how long these associations take to emerge, and whether the patterns differ across NBFC business models.

The study combines descriptive analysis of funding costs and lending growth with pooled and company-level regression analysis.

## Research Objectives

- Examine the relationship between RBI repo rate changes and NBFC loan growth.
- Investigate whether the relationship differs across contemporaneous and lagged periods.
- Compare the estimated responses of NBFCs with different business models and funding structures.
- Examine how liquidity conditions, funding risk premiums, and macroeconomic shocks complicate monetary policy transmission.
- Discuss the implications for monetary policy analysis and financial-sector regulation.

## Research Questions

1. How are changes in the RBI repo rate associated with NBFC lending growth?
2. Does the estimated relationship become stronger after a time lag?
3. Do NBFCs with different business models exhibit different response patterns?
4. What limitations arise when assessing monetary policy transmission during periods of financial stress and economic disruption?

## Data

The study uses quarterly data covering FY2019–FY2024 for five major listed Indian NBFCs:

- Bajaj Finance
- Muthoot Finance
- Cholamandalam Investment and Finance
- Shriram Finance
- Mahindra & Mahindra Financial Services

The analysis considers variables including:

- RBI repo rate
- Year-on-year loan growth
- Cost of funds
- Net interest margin (NIM)
- Gross non-performing assets (GNPA)
- GDP growth
- Consumer Price Index (CPI) inflation
- Selected indicators for major economic and financial disruptions

The dataset covers up to 120 company-quarter observations for the five firms. Loan-growth analysis uses a smaller sample of approximately 100 observations because of the availability of the required lagged and year-on-year data.

## Methodology

The research follows a quantitative, observational approach.

### 1. Descriptive Analysis

Quarterly patterns in funding costs, lending growth, and asset quality are examined to understand how NBFCs behaved across different monetary and economic conditions.

### 2. Pooled Regression Analysis

Pooled ordinary least squares (OLS) regressions examine the association between loan growth and the repo rate, including contemporaneous and lagged specifications.

### 3. Company-Level Analysis

Separate regressions are used to compare estimated relationships across the five NBFCs and explore differences associated with their business models.

### 4. Lag Analysis

The repo rate is examined at different lags to investigate whether its association with lending growth emerges gradually rather than immediately.

### 5. Asymmetry Analysis

The study explores whether lending growth responds differently during rate-hiking and rate-cutting periods. The results are treated as exploratory because the sample is limited and major economic shocks may influence the estimates.

## Key Findings

- **Funding-cost transmission:** NBFC funding costs remain above the repo rate, reflecting additional credit and liquidity risk premiums. Their movements suggest that changes in policy rates do not translate mechanically or uniformly into NBFC borrowing costs.
- **Lagged association:** The pooled regressions show a negative association between the repo rate and loan growth, with the strongest estimated association appearing at a two-quarter lag.
- **Differences across firms:** Company-level estimates vary in magnitude and direction, but none of the individual estimates is statistically significant at conventional levels in the reported specifications.
- **Economic disruptions matter:** The COVID-19 period and the subsequent recovery complicate the relationship between policy rates and lending growth.
- **Asymmetry remains inconclusive:** The available sample does not provide convincing evidence that NBFC lending responds differently to rate hikes and cuts.

These findings should be interpreted as conditional associations rather than causal effects.


## Conclusion

This research examines how monetary policy transmission to India's NBFC sector may differ from transmission through conventional banking channels. The results indicate a negative lagged association between the repo rate and NBFC lending growth, alongside variation across firms and meaningful limitations arising from the sample and macroeconomic environment.

The central implication is that NBFC monetary transmission should be assessed through funding conditions, institutional characteristics, and broader economic developments rather than through the repo rate alone.
